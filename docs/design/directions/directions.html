PLAN-design · D1.2 audit and D2 directions · 19 Oct 2026

Wayfarer feels old because it was never designed as a whole

I reviewed the app on main at three widths in both themes and wrote 36 findings. Below are the ones that matter most, what the rivals do, and three directions for the overhaul. Pick one, or a mix, and I start on the foundations.

What is wrong, worst first

Severity 1 blocks a task, 2 slows or misleads, 3 costs polish or trust. The full list with screenshots is docs/design/audit.md.

U-001 · S1

It scrolls like a web page, so the response is below the fold

At 1440 × 900 the response body starts at y = 700. Environments live under the composer at y = 1,110. Every rival is a fixed shell of full-height panes.

U-013 · S1

The run report repeats one paragraph 42 times

No pass/fail count, no progress bar, no failure filter, no diagnosis. A failed run makes the page 3,771 px tall.

U-002 · S2

Transport options sit above the request

Route, redirects, certificate checks, each with a sentence of explanation, and a "What the browser does" accordion: 250 px that most requests never touch.

U-008 · S2

Clicking a request does nothing

Opening takes a double click. Every rival opens on one click. A Postman user assumes it is broken.

U-009 · S2

The tree has no method badges

Names only, in 40 px rows. GET and POST look the same.

U-011 · S2

The command palette is a dialog of buttons

Titled modal, card rows, commands only. It cannot find a request, a collection, an environment or a history entry.

U-006 · S2

Six unlabelled toolbar icons

Lock and key are both the vault; a router glyph is the Local Bridge. Half of them vanish below 1,024 px.

U-022 · S2

Empty states offer no way in

Dashed boxes that say "nothing here". No "paste cURL", no "import from Postman", no example request.

U-015 · S2

Import review shows different names than it stores

Shows "Status And Body", stores "Status and body". One 90 px card per request.

U-004 · S3

Cards in cards in cards

11 border radii and 4 shadow styles on one screen; 8 font weights from 400 to 650; text down to 10 px; three different blues.

U-019 · S3

The Send button glows

Indigo with a coloured shadow, lavender when disabled. Reads as a 2020 landing page, not a tool used all day.

U-033 · S3

Widget layer costs more than it gives

1,490 lines of CSS and SCSS make Angular Material look like the tokens; 23 ! overrides in templates.

What the rivals teach

Postman went desktop-and-Git

v12 has Native Git: collections as files in your repo, desktop only. Free plan is one user since March 2026. Its weight is now agents, flows, catalogs: heavy for someone who wants to send one request.

Bruno and Yaak won "your files"

Plain-text collections, no account. Both are desktop installs. Yaak is the polish benchmark: quiet chrome, full-height panes, method-coloured tree, a status bar.

Hoppscotch is the only browser rival

No install, guest mode, but localhost needs an extension and its local story is partial. Wayfarer's Local Bridge and vault beat it, and the UI does not show that.

Linear set the 2026 bar for dense tools

Dimmer sidebar so content leads; fewer, smaller icons; softer separators; warmer greys. "Structure should be felt, not seen."

⌘K is navigation now

Linear, Raycast, VS Code, Zed: one floating list that finds things and runs commands, grouped, with keycaps on the right.

Explain on demand, not in advance

Rivals put transport and TLS in a request's Settings tab and say why a request failed after it fails. Wayfarer says it before anything is sent.

Three directions

Same request, same data, three characters. All three fix the layout (fixed full-height panes), the tree, the palette and the response. They differ in density, voice and look.

What it is

A native professional tool, closest to Yaak and Linear. Warm graphite neutrals, one muted green accent, hairline separators instead of cards, a quiet sidebar, a status bar that says where the request went and whether the vault is open.

What it gives up

Personality. It looks like a well-made tool, not like Wayfarer. The privacy story lives in the status bar, not in the face of the app.

Who it serves best

The solo developer and the team leaving Postman: everything sits where they expect it.

What it is

A dense, keyboard-first tool. Monospace everywhere, 22 px rows, response below the request so long URLs and long JSON both get full width. The address bar is a prompt that also shows environment and route. One loud accent, used only for Send.

What it gives up

Approachability. The occasional user and the QA tester meet a wall of mono text. Harder to keep at WCAG AA with small type.

Who it serves best

The solo backend developer who lives in a terminal and the editor.

What it is

A calmer tool that explains, but only after something happens. Larger type, top-level sections instead of hidden drawers, and a right rail that says why a request failed and what left the machine. The privacy story is visible on every request.

What it gives up

Density. Fewer rows on screen, and the rail costs 300 px a power user may want back (it collapses).

Who it serves best

The privacy-bound engineer, the QA tester and the occasional user.

What I recommend

A, with C's rail. Workbench's layout, density and quiet look, plus Field guide's "why it failed / where it went" panel as a collapsible inspector. That keeps Wayfarer familiar to Postman users and makes its real difference, privacy you can see, visible without paragraphs above the request.

Widgets: drop Angular Material for Angular Aria plus platform primitives (popover, <dialog>, anchor positioning), proven first by the D1.5 bake-off on the tree, select and menu. That deletes about 1,500 lines of override CSS.

Decisions I need from you

  1. Which direction, or which mix.
  2. Whether Material goes, decided by the D1.5 bake-off, or stays for now.
  3. Whether to keep the name and the current mark during the overhaul.
  4. Whether to start with the shell (fixed panes, tree, palette, status bar) or with the foundations (tokens, type, widgets).
